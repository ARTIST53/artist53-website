Automotive Branding · Logo Case Study

Night Owl Automotive

A complete identity refresh built around an illustrated owl, automotive hardware and custom lettering. The goal was to replace the older generic owl mark with something ownable, recognizable and flexible enough to work as both a full illustrative logo and a standalone wordmark.

01 / Starting Point

From A Generic Owl To A Custom Identity

The original Night Owl Automotive identity used a familiar owl illustration with traditional serif lettering. It communicated the business name, but it did not create a distinctive visual system that could belong to the shop alone.

The redesign kept the owl as the core idea while rebuilding it around the automotive world. A wrench, gear, diamond-plate texture and sharper custom typography gave the identity more attitude and a stronger connection to the service being offered.

02 / Identity Development

Building The Owl Around Automotive Details

The presentation explored how the custom lettering and illustrated owl could operate together and separately. Color studies tested two accent directions while black-and-white versions proved that the design could hold its structure without depending on color.

The System

The illustration carries the personality, but the custom Night Owl Automotive lettering was deliberately developed to stand on its own. That gives the brand a compact secondary logo for situations where the full owl artwork would be too detailed.

03 / Secondary Logo

A Standalone Wordmark For Flexibility

The secondary logo strips the identity down to its custom typography and small gear detail. It keeps the same visual attitude as the full mark while giving the business a simpler option for signage, small applications and layouts where the illustration is not needed.

04 / Primary Illustrative Logo

The Full Night Owl Automotive Mark

The primary logo combines the owl and the automotive language into one illustration. The face is split between industrial surfaces, the gear reinforces the mechanical theme and the wrench cuts across the lower portion of the mark. The custom wordmark locks above the illustration to complete the identity.

05 / Brand Application

Putting The New Identity To Work

The redesigned business card shows the difference between having a logo and having an identity system. The full illustrative mark becomes the visual anchor, the yellow and deep navy palette creates immediate recognition and the supporting type carries the practical information without competing with the brand.

Because the wordmark can function independently from the owl, the business has both a detailed hero mark and a simplified secondary option instead of forcing one logo configuration into every use.
